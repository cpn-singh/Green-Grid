Efficiency Metrics & Power Sourcing Models
Rather than generating power strictly on-site, Indian green data centers use a combination of strategic procurement frameworks: [1]
Open Access Power & PPAs: Operators buy power directly from clean energy grids at locked-in rates. Round-the-clock (RTC) renewable contracts average around INR 4.98 per unit, cutting data center operating costs by up to 30% compared to traditional fossil fuels or natural gas tariffs.
Firm Dispatchable Power (RTC): Clean energy firms like ReNew have deployed wind-solar-battery projects capable of delivering 24/7 uninterrupted power to meet the rigorous "always-on" criteria of mission-critical servers.
Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE): While legacy data centers in India run at a PUE of 1.8–2.0, next-generation green data centers are targeting PUE ratios of 1.2 to 1.3 via advanced liquid cooling and ambient air utilization. [1, 2, 3, 4]

These power providers are generally categorized into Utility Hyperscale Independent Power Producers (IPPs), Commercial & Industrial (C&I) Specialists, and Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (EPC) Contractors.

1. Utility Hyperscale Independent Power Producers (IPPs)

3. Utility Engineering & Component Suppliers (EPC)
For corporations looking to own their infrastructure organically rather than purchasing power through a third-party grid contract, these firms design, procure, and build the physical arrays.
Sterling and Wilson Renewable Energy: A leading global pure-play green EPC contractor specializing in utility-scale solar installations, floating solar, and high-capacity battery storage systems.
Suzlon Group: India's dominant wind energy solutions provider. Suzlon manufactures wind turbines and executes end-to-end full-stack renewable integration for data centers and large industrial campuses.
To transmit electrical energy from renewable resources into a data center, operators use a mix of financial, physical, and grid-level frameworks. Because data centers require 99.999% uptime (Tier III or IV standards) and renewable energy is intermittent (the sun doesn't always shine; the wind doesn't always blow), delivering clean power requires sophisticated sourcing models. 

1. Grid-Tied Virtual & Financial Sourcing
The data center remains physically connected to the traditional local utility grid, but its power consumption is financially or contractually tied to a specific renewable energy plant elsewhere.
Virtual Power Purchase Agreements (vPPAs): A financial structure where the data center developer agrees to a fixed price for electricity with a renewable energy developer. The renewable energy is sold directly into the local wholesale market, and the data center receives Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs) to legally offset its carbon footprint.
Green Tariffs / Green Power Programs: Local government or state-owned power distribution companies (DISCOMs) offer a specialized "green tariff" tier. The data center pays a premium on its utility bill, and the utility guarantees that it will procure an equivalent amount of renewable energy on the data center's behalf.
2. Off-Site Physical Sourcing (Wheeling & Open Access)
The data center physically consumes green energy generated at a distant location by transmitting it across third-party utility lines.
Physical Power Purchase Agreements (Physical PPAs): The data center signs a long-term contract to buy electricity from a specific off-site renewable asset (e.g., a solar farm 200 miles away). The power is physically routed through the national or state grid to the data center.
Open Access & Group Captive Power: Popular in regions like India, the data center operator sets up a joint-venture equity stake in a renewable power plant (Group Captive). Under open-access regulations, the state grid infrastructure is used to "wheel" (transmit) this power from the generation site directly to the facility, bypassing standard retail utility rates.
3. Round-the-Clock (RTC) & Hybrid Sourcing
To solve the problem of solar dropping off at night or wind dying down, modern providers use a blended approach to provide stable, uninterrupted power.
Wind-Solar Hybrid Systems: Combining wind turbines and solar arrays at the same generation site. Wind speeds are often higher at night when solar power is zero, helping smooth out the total generation curve.
Firm and Dispatchable Renewable Energy (FDRE): A multi-source setup that pairs wind/solar with Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) or Pumped Hydro Storage. Excess energy generated during peak hours is stored and discharged later, ensuring a steady, constant stream of electricity to match the data center's baseline load.
4. On-Site Generation (Behind-the-Meter)
Power is generated directly on the data center’s property, eliminating transmission lines and utility middle-men entirely.
Rooftop & Carport Solar Arrays: Photovoltaic (PV) panels installed on the roof of the data center or over parking structures. While this directly feeds clean power into the building, on-site solar usually only covers 1% to 5% of a hyperscale data center's massive power needs.
Hydrogen Fuel Cells: Emerging technology where hydrogen is stored on-site and converted into electricity via electrochemical reactions, producing only water vapor as a byproduct. These are increasingly used to replace diesel backup generators for emergency outages.
Small Modular Reactors (SMRs): A growing trend for next-generation AI data centers. Operators contract or co-locate with compact, on-site nuclear reactors to get massive, 100% carbon-free, baseline power independent of the weather.

        generation_config={"response_mime_type": "application/json"}

    return json.loads(interaction.output_text)
